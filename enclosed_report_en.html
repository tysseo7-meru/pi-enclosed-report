Enclosed-Period Suspect Address Analysis

Scope: all 11,413,664 payments of the Pi mainnet enclosed period (before the Open Network, up to 20 February 2025)

Confirmed list: 2,153 addresses (address list)

Expanded list: 31,858 addresses (address list)

Position: a sequel to Project No.1 (tracing 8,132 suspect addresses). No.1 was built from a limited window (October 2023 to February 2024); this project re-judged the whole enclosed period with the same approach. This page freezes the results before the period is extended to the end of 2025.

Summary

Judging every payment of the enclosed period against the victim conditions confirmed 2,153 suspect addresses, and expanding them the same way as in Project No.1 gave 31,858. The expansion covers 99.0% of Project No.1's list, and some 466 million Pi flowed out of the list after the period ended.

  • Judging every enclosed-period payment against the victim conditions confirmed 2,153 suspect addresses.
  • Inside the list, 2,052 addresses connect into one group and 1,432 form the mutually-paying core. Intra-list payments: 833,491 operations, about 497,828,975 Pi.
  • After the enclosed period ended (20 February 2025), about 466,001,378 Pi flowed out of the list, from 1,475 addresses (68.5% of the list), mostly toward wallets reported to belong to exchanges.
  • Of the two large hubs, teltlk is on the confirmed list. pi chain mall is not, but payments from the list flowed into it one-way (zero in reverse).
  • Expanding the confirmed list with the same method as Project No.1 gives 31,858 addresses, covering 99.0% of No.1's 8,132; 31,854 of them connect into a single group.

About the list

The entry point is payments made to addresses that once sent 1,000 Pi or more in a single payment; there were 107,705 such addresses in the enclosed period. For the 1,306,960 senders of those payments, we checked whether any single payment met all three conditions:

  • at the time of that payment, the sender had never received a payment before it;
  • the payment was made within 3 minutes after a lockup release;
  • after the payment, the sender's balance was below 2 Pi.

65,355 payments met all three conditions. The confirmed addresses are the recipients of those payments: 2,153 of them. This is the list this report covers.

The conditions capture a theft pattern: a victim whose passphrase was phished sends almost everything within minutes of a lockup release. Not matching the conditions does not mean an address is innocent. Details of the procedure are in the appendix.

The evidence for an expanded address is weaker than for a starting point: it received a large one-shot payment from a starting point or from within the expansion, and was not individually confirmed by the victim conditions. Even so, covering 99.0% of the No.1 list and connecting almost entirely into one group describes the character of this expansion as a whole.

Limitations

  • The scope is the enclosed period only. Thefts that happened just after the period ended on 20 February 2025, even days later, are structurally outside this judgment.
  • Confirmation is a lower bound; not matching the conditions is not proof of innocence. Of 2,524,130 candidate payments, 12 (from 2 victims) could not be retrieved and were never judged.
  • Outflows in Result 2 can include ordinary sales. Exchange labels rest on third-party reports.
  • The expansion in Result 4 rests on weaker evidence than the starting point (see Result 4).

Appendix: Method

Data: the Pi mainnet public records (Horizon API) — all 11,413,664 enclosed-period payments, plus lockup release times and balance histories. For payments sent to the 107,705 addresses that once sent 1,000 Pi or more at once, we counted payments where the sender had no incoming before that payment, sent within 3 minutes of either kind of lockup release, and was left with under 2 Pi; the recipients of those payments are the confirmed list. Connections and outflows count payments inside the list and payments leaving it after 20 February 2025.

How much of the Project No.1 list was confirmed, by layer

Of Project No.1's 8,132 addresses, the share confirmed by this judgment was 7.1% overall: 65.8% for the 278 first confirmed, 42.3% for expansion ring 1, and 2.9% for ring 2. Not being confirmed does not mean innocent: addresses that never entered the judgment were never judged.
